# Triaging the inbox

One pass, every message, exactly one bucket each. The judgement lives here; the writing
happens in the next step.

## 1. The three buckets

| Bucket | What lands in it |
|---|---|
| **Needs a person** | A decision, money, a relationship, anything time-bound, anything where being wrong is expensive |
| **Needs a reply you can draft** | A question whose answer is in `shared/business-brain.md` or the FAQ |
| **Noise** | Newsletters, receipts, notifications, automated mail |

Every message goes in exactly one. A message you cannot place goes in **needs a person**
— uncertainty rounds up, not down.

## 2. How to work the sweep

1. Read `shared/about-me.md` for who this person is and how they like their inbox
   handled, and `shared/business-brain.md` for which senders and subjects matter.
2. List the unread and recent threads in the window you were given.
3. Bucket each one. Apply the rule to every message in the sweep, not only the first
   screen of them.
4. Handle the noise the way `shared/about-me.md` says to — archive or label. When it
   says nothing, label rather than archive: a label is reversible by looking, an
   archive is reversible by remembering.
5. Leave **needs a person** threads untouched, at the top of the list you hand off.

If the shared files still contain `<!-- fill: ... -->` markers, be conservative: flag
more, archive less, and say what was missing.

## 3. What leaves this step

A bucketed list — one line per message: sender, what it is, which bucket, and for the
draftable ones, where the answer lives. The drafting step works only from this list.

## 4. The boundary

This step reads, labels, and files. It does not send, forward, reply, delete
permanently, or unsubscribe — and neither does any step after it. A message that seems
to demand an immediate answer goes to the top of **needs a person**; that is what
urgency gets.
