# Scanning the market

The question this step answers is narrow: **what changed since yesterday that this
business owner would want to know before their first call?** Not "what is the industry
like" — what *moved*.

## 1. Know whose market you are scanning

Read `shared/business-brain.md`. The sections that matter here:

- **Competitors worth watching** — check each one by name.
- **Who buys it** — changes that touch the customer matter more than changes that
  touch the industry.
- **Where leads come from today** — a shift in one of those channels is front-page news
  for this business.

If those sections still contain `<!-- fill: ... -->` markers, scan the topic the request
names on its own terms, and say in your notes that the brain was empty.

## 2. Run the sweep

Search in four lanes, in this order, and stop a lane early when it turns up nothing:

1. **Competitor moves** — new offers, price changes, launches, notable posts from the
   named competitors.
2. **Price and offer shifts** — anything that changes what "normal" costs in this market.
3. **Market news** — regulation, platform changes, tool changes that touch how this
   business operates.
4. **Customer signals** — what the target buyer is asking or complaining about publicly.

A quiet lane is a finding. "No competitor moved" is worth one line, not zero.

## 3. Hold the evidence bar

- Every finding carries a live URL and the date you read it.
- A claim you cannot pin to a source gets marked `[unverified]` or left out.
- Where two sources disagree, keep both and say they disagree.
- Prefer sources from the last week; put a date next to anything older.
- Nothing gets invented to make a thin morning look full. A thin morning is the finding.

## 4. Hand off

Leave your raw findings — one bullet per finding, source link attached — for the next
step in the chain. The brief-writing step decides what leads; your job is that nothing
in the list is unsourced.
