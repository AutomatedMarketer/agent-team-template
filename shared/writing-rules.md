# Writing rules

The Content agent follows these. So does any agent drafting something a customer reads.

**Two kinds of rule live in this file, and they do not travel the same way.**

- **Voice** — *Voice in three words*, *Sentences I would actually say*, *Words and phrases I use*,
  *Words and phrases that are not mine*. These apply to **everything** the team writes, whatever
  the shape of it.
- **Format** — *Formatting I prefer* and *Call to action I default to*. Answer these for the thing
  you mostly publish. An agent writing something else — a research memo, an internal note, a reply
  to a customer — keeps your voice and **drops format rules that plainly belong to another shape**.

That line matters because the file says it governs anything a customer reads. Without the split,
an answer like *"posts under 150 words, no bulleted lists"* gets applied to a two-page research
memo, where following it is worse than ignoring it.

**Fill the samples first.** *Sentences I would actually say* is the section that does the work.
Three words describe a voice; the samples **are** the voice. It sits second here only because the
file reads better in that order once it is filled — it is not the order to answer them in.
